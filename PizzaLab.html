Timing assumes a 3–4°C fridge and 19–21°C room. Fermentation depends on actual dough activity: use the readiness checks rather than the clock alone.
Friday evening

04 · Make the poolish

Ready-to-use poolish: bubbly, fermented aroma, visibly risen or slightly receded. A slight collapse after refrigeration is fine; sharp solvent-like aroma or strong liquid separation suggests overfermentation.
Saturday afternoon

05 · Mix the final dough

Choose your method

Hand-mixed method shown. See interactive app for stand mixer variation.

Target dough temperature: 21–24°C. Use cool mixing water as needed. Stop kneading when smooth, cohesive and stretchy. Avoid repeatedly tightening the whole dough or overworking it.
Sunday morning

06 · Divide, ball and proof

Ready-to-open test: balls are slightly spread and airy; a gentle finger press springs back slowly and partly; the dough stretches without fighting back. If still tight, allow more covered rest.
Sunday noon

07 · Ooni setup & baking

Thin-base rule: go light on sauce and cheese. Stretch the center thin without tearing; preserve a rim of approximately 1.5–2 cm. Adjust the flame if the top cooks too quickly compared with the bottom.
Optional

08 · Margherita toppings

Use uncooked passata with salt, fully drained mozzarella, basil and olive oil. Toppings scale with the pizza count, not ball weight.

Reference notes

How to get the intended result

Thin and soft

Approximately 25–28 cm (10–11 in) for a 200 g dough ball. Press air from center to rim and stretch gently.

Temperature

Stone surface 400–430°C; preheat around 30–45 minutes and check with an infrared thermometer.

Baking window

Generally 70–120 seconds; turn frequently once the base sets. Wait for stone recovery between pizzas.

Room temperature

At ~19°C the dough may need more proof time; at 23–24°C it can be ready significantly sooner.
